Brief: art direction

Question being decided

Which art style should the whole game be built in? The customer wants "nice graphics and sound" and everything generated by us (no external assets, no fonts from CDNs, no image files; see addendum in ../../00-brief.md). The style choice fixes the renderer design, the asset pipeline (all drawn in code), the lighting approach, the HUD look and the effort of every later art task, so it is cheaper to decide now than after the first slice.

Requirements involved

  • R-070 health and mana gauges (here drawn as glass globes with a liquid level; R-070's office-vessel idea, e.g. coffee cup / water jug, is not yet applied)
  • R-071 skill bar (5 icon slots plus one empty slot, XP bar, level)
  • R-112 HTML5 canvas 2D renderer, no plug-ins
  • NFR-A03 colour-blind-safe loot and UI (rarity is shown by colour, marker shape, border style and beam shape)
  • NFR-L01 no third-party names, assets or text (everything on screen is drawn procedurally; names and items are original)
  • NFR performance target: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop (see "Performance" below)
  • Sound (R-080) is not part of this topic; it is a separate synthesis question.

What all three variants share (same content, different style)

Each file is one <canvas> living style tile, animated with requestAnimationFrame:

  • an isometric 2:1 slice of an office level gone wrong: carpet tiles, cubicle partition walls (one collapsed, one doorway), two desks with glowing monitors, a water cooler, a filing cabinet, a hanging fluorescent fixture that flickers and casts a light pool, floor cables, a coffee puddle, stains, scattered paper
  • the hero (office worker, tie, lanyard, keyboard-sword) with a breathing/bob idle and a swing every 2.0 s (wind-up, strike, recover)
  • three monsters: a shambling "Zombie Intern", a hovering "Printer Demon" (paper wings, cable tentacles, orbiting sheets, chomping tray), a hulking "Middle Manager" (elite, binder club)
  • three loot drops, each with a floor ring, light beam, floating label and sparkles: Magic "Sturdy Lanyard of Focus" (blue, diamond marker, plain border, thin beam), Rare "Ergonomic Chair of Haste" (yellow, star marker, double border, wide beam), Unique "The Corner Office Keycard" (orange, hexagon marker, notched double border, widest beam with rising chevrons)
  • a hit effect when the swing lands: flash, damage number, spark and paper-scrap burst, slash arc, target recoil and flash
  • bottom HUD: health and mana globes with an animated liquid surface, skill bar with drawn icons (stapler strike, reply-all, coffee break, mute shield, meeting invite), a cooldown sweep on slot 3, XP bar, level
  • lighting: darkness with a light radius around the hero, the flickering fluorescent pool, monitor glows, cooler, demon and loot glows

How to view

Open A.html, B.html or C.html directly from disk. Everything is inline; no network. ?frame=N renders deterministic frame N (60 frames per second of scene time) and stops animating; this is for screenshots. Good frames: ?frame=92 (just after the hero's hit lands: damage number, particles, flash), ?frame=20 (idle), ?frame=70 (sword raised in wind-up). window.__ready is set after the first frame. The attack loop is 2.0 s; the hit lands at 1.42 s of each cycle.

Common technique (so the comparison is fair)

Same scene description and animation code in all three; only the renderer differs (materials, shading, outlines, lighting, HUD, text). Static geometry (floor, walls, furniture, decals) is drawn once into a cached layer and re-drawn only on resize. Responsive from 390 px wide up; the scene scales with the window and the HUD scales with the viewport height.

Performance

Measured in headless Chromium with software rendering (no GPU, 1440x900, worst case): A and C about 14 ms of frame work, B about 4 ms. With a GPU-backed canvas on a laptop this should be well inside 16 ms, but it has not been measured on real hardware (open question for the Spec phase). B is cheapest because it renders 320x180 pixels and scales the result.

Notes: art direction variants

How to compare: open the three files at the same window size. Same content, same animation; only the art style changes.

A: Gritty gothic office (A.html)

  • What is different: dark desaturated palette, painterly gradient shading with a thin dark stroke, carpet fibre texture and grime baked into the floor, heavy vignette and real darkness around the hero. Warm candle-like amber monitor light against a sickly green-white fluorescent pool and a red demon glow; additive light bloom, dust motes, serif type, riveted iron-and-glass globes.
  • Suits: the "moody dungeon-crawling" feel of the genre; players who value atmosphere over readability.
  • Trade-offs: hardest to keep readable (dark, low contrast; monsters can sink into the gloom, so gameplay needs rim light or outline helpers); most fill-rate heavy (gradients, additive lights, full-screen darkness layer); every new sprite needs careful shading to avoid looking muddy.
  • Effort for a whole game: L. Many more art decisions per asset, plus lighting performance work and tuning for readability.

B: Crisp pixel art (B.html)

  • What is different: the whole frame is a 320x180 index buffer rasterised by our own code (no canvas antialiasing), 25-colour palette, integer scaling with nearest-neighbour, light falloff by ordered (Bayer) dithering with palette-ramp darkening and warm/cool tints, a built-in 3x5 bitmap font, pixel globes and pixel icons. Rarity uses colour plus marker shape plus border style.
  • Suits: a retro-genre audience, tiny build and runtime budget, very predictable look at any window size (integer scale), easiest to keep consistent.
  • Trade-offs: small sprites limit expression (the hero is about 35 pixels tall); text is blocky and all caps; portrait phones get a small crisp image with margins, not a larger crop; hand-tuning of sprites will eventually beat the procedural primitives used in this tile (it needs a pixel-sprite pipeline: either hand-written sprite arrays or a small procedural pixel-drawing library).
  • Effort for a whole game: M. The rendering is cheap and consistent, but sprite animation frames and the palette discipline need steady care.

C: Stylised vector (C.html)

  • What is different: bold dark outlines, flat colours with one shade step per surface, rim light on up-left edges, oversized heads and cartoony proportions, banded (toon) lighting instead of smooth gradients, saturated accents on a dark indigo base, flat glossy globes, rounded sans-serif UI with outlined text.
  • Suits: best readability at a glance (silhouettes, rarity and enemies pop), friendly and humorous office tone, scales to any resolution without blur, easiest style to extend with new monsters and items because shapes are built from a few primitives.
  • Trade-offs: least "dark dungeon" atmosphere of the three (the tone is darker than it first looks, but moodiness comes from colour, not light); outlines cost fill-rate; risks looking generic unless the palette and proportions are kept distinctive.
  • Effort for a whole game: M. New assets are cheap; the cost is keeping outline width, shade rules and rim-light rules consistent.

Cross-cutting findings

  • All three met the stated constraints: single file, no external assets or fonts, drawn in code, original names, 390 px wide up, ?frame=N, window.__ready.
  • File sizes: A about 49 KB, B about 44 KB, C about 47 KB (the harness rule of ~60 KB is met).
  • All three share one scene engine (scene description, animation, hit effect, lighting inputs). A real game would keep that split: a style-independent scene and a swappable renderer. That makes a later style change (or a high-contrast accessibility mode) cheaper.
  • Performance is likely fine for all three on a laptop with a GPU-backed canvas, but only measured in software rendering (see brief.md). A and C each use two half-resolution light layers composed once per frame; the full game needs a test with 50 monsters (NFR).
  • Not covered: sound, character art beyond the one hero, tile variety, fonts for localisation, large-screen performance at 4K.

Copy of C with the combined round-1 review fixes (review-r1-appeal.md, review-r1-craft.md). A, B, C and decision.md are unchanged.

  • Tone: world graded dark with A's lighting model. One baked mask holds a 90 % darkness layer, a toon-banded hero-radius light, a heavier vignette, and coloured pools (cold fluorescent, cyan monitor glow, red emergency lamp and demon glow, loot glow). Blood decals, wall smears and tile scuffs added. Outlines, flat colours and silhouettes are kept.

File size 58 KB (limit about 60 KB).
  • Effort for a whole game: M, unchanged from C; the lighting bake and the placement pass are reusable engine parts.

01 Core loop and controls

QF-01-1 Town stairs: floor 1 or deepest reached floor?

PROXY (high): The town stairs lead down to the deepest reached floor, via an elevator in the Lobby. This is the genre's waypoint idea, re-expressed in office terms. A first-time player has "deepest reached = 1". config.townStairsTarget = 'deepest'. Basis: R-004 ("come back to the deepest reached floor"); genre convention (waypoint / return to depth); circle-01 Q7 (short sessions).

QF-01-2 Click on an unreachable tile (wall)?

PROXY (medium): The player walks to the nearest reachable tile to the click, as the genre classic did. noPath is raised only when no tile closer than the current one is reachable. Basis: genre convention (brief: stay close to the original; customer's words silent).

QF-01-3 Skill panel key: S, K or both?

PROXY (medium): Both bound by default (S and K open the skill panel). Basis: spec proposal; costs nothing; customer silent.

QF-01-4 Collision of skill hotkeys 1-4 and belt potions 1-4

PROXY (high): Resolved as the genre classic did. Belt potions on keys 1-4. Skills on the mouse: left button = move/attack, right button = the currently selected skill. Conventions

  • Status: only the user's explicit words (00-brief.md, verbatim) are agreed. Everything else is draft.
  • Provenance: OBSERVED = seen in evidence; INFERRED = derived from the brief or from known genre conventions; ASSUMED = my guess, needs an answer. Greenfield: there is no as-is model. "Genre knowledge" is recorded as INFERRED (from the brief's "stay close to the original"), not OBSERVED.
  • Scope: MVP = first playable slice set; LATER = after MVP. Priority is must / should / could within that scope.
  • Naming rule (claim G2): the original's names, art, text, music and story are never used. Refer to it only as "the original".
  • Acceptance criteria are not written yet (Spec phase). Items marked "AC: pending" need them before leaving Circles.

Non-functional requirements (working draft, circle 1)

Date: 2026-10-13. All items are draft. No NFR comes from the user's words; provenance is shown per item. Values marked ASSUMED need an answer or confirmation.

Performance

NFR-P01 Frame rate

  • Target: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop (integrated graphics, 4 cores, 8 GB RAM) with up to 50 active monsters on screen; never below 30 fps in the same scene.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-P02 Load time

  • Target: playable within 5 s on a broadband connection from a cold cache; total download at most 10 MB (stretch: 3 MB).
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-P03 Floor generation time

  • Target: a new floor generates in under 500 ms on the reference laptop.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-P04 Input latency

  • Target: click-to-visible-reaction under 100 ms at 60 fps.
  • Provenance: INFERRED (genre feel) | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-P05 Memory

  • Target: under 500 MB of browser memory after a one-hour session (no unbounded growth from items, particles or floors).
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

Browsers and platforms

NFR-B01 Supported browsers

  • Latest two stable versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop (Windows, macOS, Linux). Only standard web APIs; no plug-ins.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED (brief says "browser") | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-B02 Screen size

  • Playable from 1280x720 up to 4K; the view scales; minimum window width 1024 px. Mobile and tablet are out of scope for MVP.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-B03 No install, no account, no server needed to play

  • Provenance: INFERRED from brief ("browser") | Priority: must | Status: draft

Offline

NFR-O01 Works offline after first load

  • The game runs without network once loaded (all assets local, no CDN at run time). Saves are local. A service worker for installable offline use is "could".
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-O02 No telemetry or network calls during play

  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

Accessibility

NFR-A01 Remappable keys

  • All keyboard bindings can be changed in options and persist in the save/options. Mouse buttons stay as defaults in MVP.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED (not the user's words) | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-A02 Pause

  • The game can be paused at any time outside menus with Esc; simulation and audio stop; panels may be opened while paused. No time-limited input anywhere.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-A03 Colour-blind-safe loot and UI

  • Rarity is never shown by colour alone: each rarity also has a distinct label prefix/icon/border pattern. Palette checked for deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia. Health vs mana distinguished by shape/fill too.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-A04 Text size and contrast

  • UI text at least 14 px at 1080p with a size option; contrast ratio at least 4.5:1 for body text.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-A05 Reduced motion / flashing

  • Option to reduce screen shake and flash effects; no content flashing more than 3 times per second.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

NFR-A06 Language

  • English only in MVP; all text kept in one string table so translation is possible later (i18n "could").
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

Legal and IP guardrails

NFR-L01 No third-party names, assets or text

  • No names, characters, artwork, sprites, sounds, music, logos, level layouts copied from the original or any other third-party game or brand. No asset downloaded from the internet unless its licence is recorded and it is CC0 or equivalent; MVP default is that all assets are generated by agents in code.
  • Provenance: INFERRED (claim G2, harness rule 06) | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-L02 Mechanics are free to follow the genre

  • Genre mechanics (click-to-move, loot rarity tiers, inventory grid, potion belt, procedural levels) are ideas and systems, not protected expression; used as general genre conventions. This is an engineering position, not legal advice; see circle-01 Q1.
  • Provenance: INFERRED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-L03 Title, store/marketing text and trade dress

  • The title and any public description must not include the original's name, nor imply endorsement. No lookalike logo, font or colour trade dress of the original. Public release is not delegated (see brief).
  • Provenance: INFERRED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-L04 Dependencies

  • Any library used has a permissive licence (MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0, ISC); licence list shipped with the build.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-L05 No real brands, companies or people in game text

  • Satire stays generic.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

Save data and retention

NFR-D01 Data stored

  • Only the game save and options, in browser local storage on the player's machine. No personal data, no accounts, no cookies, no analytics. GDPR/personal-data scope: none by design.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-D02 Retention

  • Save stays until the player deletes it (in-game "delete character" or clearing browser data). No expiry. Browsers may evict storage, so export/import (R-092) exists.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-D03 Save format versioning

  • Saves carry a version number; a newer game version migrates older saves or refuses with a clear message; a corrupt save never crashes the game (offers a fresh start and keeps the bad file for export).
  • Provenance: INFERRED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-D04 Save integrity

  • Players may edit their own save; no anti-cheat in MVP.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: could | Status: draft

Security baseline

NFR-S01 Static content only

  • No server code, no user-supplied content executed; test-mode hooks (R-121, R-123) are off in normal play unless a documented flag is set. Content-Security-Policy where hosting allows.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft

Reliability and quality

NFR-Q01 Determinism

  • Given a seed and an input sequence, the simulation produces identical state on every supported browser (see R-120). Rendering is excluded.
  • Provenance: INFERRED | Priority: must | Status: draft

NFR-Q02 No crash on invalid state

  • Errors are caught at the game loop; the game shows an error screen with a way to export the save.
  • Provenance: ASSUMED | Priority: should | Status: draft
