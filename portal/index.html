What is being decided

Brief: art direction

Question being decided

Which art style should the whole game be built in? The customer wants "nice graphics and sound" and everything generated by us (no external assets, no fonts from CDNs, no image files; see addendum in ../../00-brief.md). The style choice fixes the renderer design, the asset pipeline (all drawn in code), the lighting approach, the HUD look and the effort of every later art task, so it is cheaper to decide now than after the first slice.

Requirements involved

  • R-070 health and mana gauges (here drawn as glass globes with a liquid level; R-070's office-vessel idea, e.g. coffee cup / water jug, is not yet applied)
  • R-071 skill bar (5 icon slots plus one empty slot, XP bar, level)
  • R-112 HTML5 canvas 2D renderer, no plug-ins
  • NFR-A03 colour-blind-safe loot and UI (rarity is shown by colour, marker shape, border style and beam shape)
  • NFR-L01 no third-party names, assets or text (everything on screen is drawn procedurally; names and items are original)
  • NFR performance target: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop (see "Performance" below)
  • Sound (R-080) is not part of this topic; it is a separate synthesis question.

What all three variants share (same content, different style)

Each file is one <canvas> living style tile, animated with requestAnimationFrame:

  • an isometric 2:1 slice of an office level gone wrong: carpet tiles, cubicle partition walls (one collapsed, one doorway), two desks with glowing monitors, a water cooler, a filing cabinet, a hanging fluorescent fixture that flickers and casts a light pool, floor cables, a coffee puddle, stains, scattered paper
  • the hero (office worker, tie, lanyard, keyboard-sword) with a breathing/bob idle and a swing every 2.0 s (wind-up, strike, recover)
  • three monsters: a shambling "Zombie Intern", a hovering "Printer Demon" (paper wings, cable tentacles, orbiting sheets, chomping tray), a hulking "Middle Manager" (elite, binder club)

A: Gritty gothic office (A.html)

  • What is different: dark desaturated palette, painterly gradient shading with a thin dark stroke, carpet fibre texture and grime baked into the floor, heavy vignette and real darkness around the hero.   • Trade-offs: hardest to keep readable (dark, low contrast; monsters can sink into the gloom, so gameplay needs rim light or outline helpers); most fill-rate heavy (gradients, additive lights, full-screen darkness layer); every new sprite needs careful shading to avoid looking muddy.
  • Effort for a whole game: L. Many more art decisions per asset, plus lighting performance work and tuning for readability.

B: Crisp pixel art (B.html)

  • What is different: the whole frame is a 320x180 index buffer rasterised by our own code (no canvas antialiasing), 25-colour palette, integer scaling with nearest-neighbour, light falloff by ordered (Bayer) dithering with palette-ramp darkening and warm/cool tints, a built-in 3x5 bitmap font, pixel globes and pixel icons. Rarity uses colour plus marker shape plus border style.
  • Suits: a retro-genre audience, tiny build and runtime budget, very predictable look at any window size (integer scale), easiest to keep consistent.

  • Effort for a whole game: M. The rendering is cheap and consistent, but sprite animation frames and the palette discipline need steady care.

C: Stylised vector (C.html)

  • What is different: bold dark outlines, flat colours with one shade step per surface, rim light on up-left edges, oversized heads and cartoony proportions, banded (toon) lighting instead of smooth gradients, saturated accents on a dark indigo base, flat glossy globes, rounded sans-serif UI with outlined text.

  • Trade-offs: least "dark dungeon" atmosphere of the three (the tone is darker than it first looks, but moodiness comes from colour, not light); outlines cost fill-rate; risks looking generic unless the palette and proportions are kept distinctive.
  • Effort for a whole game: M. New assets are cheap; the cost is keeping outline width, shade rules and rim-light rules consistent.

Brief: sound direction

Question being decided

Which sonic character should the game's synthesised audio have? The customer asked for "nice sound and graphics" and "generate what you need" (addendum in ../../00-brief.md); the proxy answer to Q5 (../../circles/circle-01.md) chose procedural sound effects plus a generative ambient loop per zone with volume sliders (../../30-spec/08-audio.md). Nothing may be a recorded file or a third-party sample (NFR-L01): every sound is built at runtime with Web Audio. The choice fixes the synthesis toolbox, the mixing rules, the CPU budget and the look-and-feel promise of the audio, so it is cheaper to decide before slice S3.

Reviewers cannot listen, so each board has a measurement mode (below) and the decision should still be made by ear.

Requirements involved

  • R-080 sound effects for core actions (attack, hit, death, pickup, UI click, level up, potion; QF-08-1 asks about miss, monster attacks and stairs, which all boards cover)
  • R-081 generative ambient per zone (lobby, open-plan floors, basement)
  • R-082 master, effects and ambient sliders plus mute (all three boards have them, wired to kit.setVolume and kit.setMuted)
  • NFR-L01 no third-party assets: no audio files, no samples, no fetches
  • NFR performance (CPU-cheap voices; see "Budget")
  • QF-08-4 "nice sound" bar: this topic is the listening gate, with the numbers below as a safety net

What all three variants share

  • One file per variant (A.html, B.html, C.html), inline CSS and JS, opened from disk, in the C2 visual style (dark indigo panels, gold trim, teal buttons, cyan highlights).
  • The same 27 sound buttons: hero swing (stapler-mace), hit flesh, hit paper, miss; hurt and death for zombie intern, printer imp and middle manager; hero hurt, low-health heartbeat (one beat pair; the game repeats it), potion (coffee slurp); gold coins, item pickup, rare sparkle, unique sparkle, level-up fanfare; Stapler Throw, Reply-All burst, Coffee Break heal, Mute; UI click, open drawer, equip, stairs, elevator ding.
  • Three ambient loops, one at a time: open-plan floors (fluorescent hum, distant printer, HVAC), basement (server fans, drips, low drones), lobby (muffled elevator-music-like pad, safe and warm). They are continuous generative beds (sustained oscillators and noise plus seeded random events), so there is no loop seam by construction (AC-R-081-3).
  • Master, effects and ambient sliders, a mute switch, and a live waveform and spectrum view. Browsers need a gesture, so the first button press starts audio.
  • A liftable createSoundKit(ctx, destination[, {seed}]) section (marked in each file), pure Web Audio and no DOM: kit.play(id, when), kit.startAmbient(zone, when[, {duration}]), kit.setVolume(bus, 0..1), kit.setMuted(bool). Chain: voice -> effects or ambient bus -> master -> compressor (20:1, -4 dB threshold) -> soft ceiling at 0.97 -> destination. Every sound has a calibrated trim.
  • Noise is seeded and generated once per context, so renders are repeatable (AC-R-080-2 style).

Budget (same rules for all)

Short SFX at most 1 s, at most about 12 Web Audio nodes per SFX, no clipping, peaks between -6 and -1 dBFS, ambient RMS between -30 and -20 dBFS, RMS spread across SFX at most 12 dB.

How to view and measure

  • Open A.html, B.html or C.html and press buttons.
  • ?measure=1 renders every SFX (1.7 s window) and 10 s of every ambient through an OfflineAudioContext (44.1 kHz stereo, all volumes at 1.0) and writes window.__report, then sets window.__ready. Per sound: duration, peak dBFS, RMS dBFS, crest factor, spectral centroid (2048-point FFT, Hann), clipping count, and for SFX the node count and render time. The report also carries a method string and a summary with the pass checks. The saved runs are measure-A.json, measure-B.json, measure-C.json (Playwright, Chromium).
  • Duration is measured to the last sample above -50 dBFS, so it includes any reverb tail. Peak and clipping cover the whole render; SFX RMS and centroid cover the active window; ambient RMS and centroid cover seconds 1 to 10.
  • Per-sound trims were set by a calibration pass (target RMS about -16 to -17 dBFS, peak limited to -5.2..-2.2 dBFS) and are stored in the kit; the numbers therefore say the mix is consistent, not that the sounds are pleasant.

  • Risks: it commits the whole game to a retro-pixel sound identity while the art is smooth vector; square waves are tiring at length and loud for their peak (RMS about 6 dB above the others at equal peak); brightness is high (centroids 1.0 to 10.8 kHz) so it can sound harsh on headphones; ambient stays "musical" instead of spooky.

  • Effort: S to M (the toolbox is small and the sounds are short note tables).

  • Character: realistic-ish and tactile. Date: 2026-10-13. Answerer: customer-proxy, under the delegation record in 00-brief.md. Source: the "Questions for the facilitator" sections of 30-spec/01 to 30-spec/10. All answers are PROXY and provisional until the real customer reviews them (proxy-log.md). Tie-breaker used where the customer's words are silent: "stay close to the original" for mechanics (brief, verbatim), read as the genre classic's conventions, never its names or assets. Answers do not change any earlier customer or proxy answer (circle-01 Q2-Q7); where one touches an earlier answer it says so.

Answer format: PROXY (confidence) / basis. "ESCALATE" = needs the real customer.

01 Core loop and controls

QF-01-1 Town stairs: floor 1 or deepest reached floor?

PROXY (high): The town stairs lead down to the deepest reached floor, via an elevator in the Lobby. This is the genre's waypoint idea, re-expressed in office terms. A first-time player has "deepest reached = 1". config.townStairsTarget = 'deepest'. Basis: R-004 ("come back to the deepest reached floor"); genre convention (waypoint / return to depth); circle-01 Q7 (short sessions).

QF-01-2 Click on an unreachable tile (wall)?

PROXY (medium): The player walks to the nearest reachable tile to the click, as the genre classic did. noPath is raised only when no tile closer than the current one is reachable. Basis: genre convention (brief: stay close to the original; customer's words silent).

QF-01-3 Skill panel key: S, K or both?

PROXY (medium): Both bound by default (S and K open the skill panel). Basis: spec proposal; costs nothing; customer silent.

QF-01-4 Collision of skill hotkeys 1-4 and belt potions 1-4

PROXY (high): Resolved as the genre classic did. Belt potions on keys 1-4. Skills on the mouse: left button = move/attack, right button = the currently selected skill. A skill selector assigns four skills to hotkeys F1-F4 (default; rebindable); pressing F1-F4 selects the skill that the right mouse button then uses. The game calls preventDefault for F1-F4 so the browser's own function keys do not fire. R-005's "hotkeys for skills (1-4)" is read as "four skill hotkeys", not the number row. Basis: brief "stay close to the original" (mechanics); R-005 "skills on mouse buttons"; R-056 (belt 1-4); no conflict with circle-01 answers. Consequence: AC-R-015-3 uses F1-F4.

QF-01-5 R-006 "meaningful in 20-40 minutes"

PROXY (medium): Accept as a release-gate measurement, not an AC: the playtest bot reaches the boss in 20-40 minutes of simulated time (tick / tickHz) on seed 1, after a balance pass. Basis: circle-01 Q7 (answer A, 20-40 min run); the measurement is the proxy's way to make Q7 testable.

QF-01-6 Rebinding collision

PROXY (medium): Reject with a notice (ui.notice), options.keys unchanged; the player must free the key first. Basis: spec proposal; safest for accessibility (NFR-A01); the genre classic is not clear on this and the customer is silent.

02 Combat

QF-02-1 Hit chance formula and clamp

PROXY (medium): Owned by the architect (ADR). Genre-style guidance: chance depends on attacker rating against defender defence and level, clamped to a floor and ceiling (recommended 5% to 95%, as in the genre). Values live in config. The spec asserts monotonicity and bounds only. Basis: genre convention; customer silent.

QF-02-2 Respawn with full health and mana?

PROXY (high): Yes, the player respawns in town with full health and mana, no gold loss. Basis: circle-01 Q7 answer A ("moderately forgiving, death returns to town with no gold loss").

QF-02-3 Dying with an item on the cursor

PROXY (medium): The item returns to the inventory; if the inventory is full it drops at the death tile on the floor (retrievable per QF-04-6). It is never destroyed. Basis: spec proposal; Q7 forgiving.

QF-02-4 Natural regeneration

PROXY (low): Genre feel: no natural health regeneration on floors; slow natural mana regeneration, rate in config and tunable by playtest. Full restore of health on the healer (QF-04-2). Both resources are refilled on respawn. Basis: genre convention (customer silent); to be reviewed after playtest.

QF-02-5 "Feel" criteria (animation, readability)

PROXY (high): Accept screenshot review by the independent visual reviewer as the gate. Basis: circle-01 Q2 (art quality via independent visual review).

03 Monsters and AI

QF-03-1 Final monster names

PROXY (medium) for working names; ESCALATE for final names. ACs test working names. All names must be original and pass the IP denylist (QF-10-2). Final names, as part of the product's naming and brand, are for the real customer. Basis: R-030 "open to the user"; IP/branding is not delegated.

QF-03-2 Which archetypes flee, at what health?

PROXY (medium): The small/weak melee archetypes of the swarm kind flee below 25% health, regroup, and return after a delay (a genre convention). Ranged and caster types and bosses never flee. Exact threshold and archetype mapping in config, set by the spec-writer within this rule. Basis: genre convention.

QF-03-3 Leash and losing interest

PROXY (medium): Monsters lose interest when the player is farther than twice their aggro radius for a set time (config), then walk back to their origin and idle. Basis: genre convention; R-032 needs no break in behaviour.

QF-03-4 "Better drops" for champions

PROXY (medium): Accept the proposal: a champion's drops are at least magic rarity (guaranteed); the mean-rarity AC stays. Basis: spec proposal.

QF-03-5 Safe radius around the entry stair

PROXY (high): Add as a requirement: no monster spawns within a safe radius of the entry stair (radius in config, suggested 8 tiles). Basis: genre convention and fairness (Q7 "forgiving").

QF-03-6 Archetype to behaviour mapping, availability by zone

PROXY (low): Delegated to the spec-writer and architect within these limits: 6 to 8 archetypes; each of the 3 behaviours used by at least two archetypes; zone 1 (floors 1-4) has about half of them, zone 2 (floors 5-8) adds the rest and keeps some of zone 1 at higher level. Not a customer-facing decision. Basis: R-031; customer silent.

05 Items and loot

QF-05-1 Container kinds

PROXY (medium): Two kinds per floor: breakable "supply crates" (gold, potions) and openable "filing cabinets" (items, better chance). Count per floor in config (suggested 4 to 10, by room size). Basis: R-050; genre convention (barrels and chests, re-expressed).

QF-05-2 Unique item dropping twice

PROXY (low): A unique drops at most once per game; a roll that would repeat a taken unique falls back to a rare of the same base. Basis: genre convention; flag for review since the spec proposed "may drop again".

QF-05-3 "Same stat group" for affix exclusion

PROXY (medium): The group is the affix's primary stat family (for example all tiers of +strength are one group, all +fire resist another). The architect lists the groups in config; an item never has two affixes of the same group. Basis: R-051; genre convention.

QF-05-4 Two-handed weapons

PROXY (medium): Yes, a few. A two-handed weapon occupies the weapon and off-hand slots; equipping one returns the off-hand item to the inventory, and is refused (with a notice) when the inventory has no room. Basis: genre convention.

QF-05-5 Potion at full health

PROXY (low): As the genre classic did, the potion is consumed (and wasted). Behind config.consumePotionAtFull so it is a one-line change if playtest shows accidental waste. Basis: genre convention; flagged because Q7 asks for "forgiving", so the real customer may prefer "refused".

QF-05-6 Selling equipped items

PROXY (high): Only inventory items can be sold; unequip first. Basis: spec proposal; genre convention.

QF-05-7 Balance table owner (drop chances, rarity weights, prices, potion values)

PROXY (high): The architect and implementer own it. All balance values live in a single config module, tuned by the playtest bots (QF-01-5). The customer sets only the feel targets already given (20-40 minutes, forgiving). Basis: customer's earlier answers delegate mechanics to "stay close to the original"; Q7.

QF-05-8 Item label and icon readability

PROXY (high): Visual review item, not an AC. Basis: circle-01 Q2.

Q2 How is a skill tree built?

  • Why it matters: the biggest design lever; it defines the skill panel, data format, tests and balance.
  • Options:
    • A. 3 branches (tabs) per class, about 10 skills per class (3 + 3 + 4), 3 tiers per branch, 1 skill point per level (R-013, 29 points at the level cap of 30), max skill level 5 (single level for capstones and some passives), prerequisites: a character level per tier (for example tiers at levels 1, 8, 16) plus one earlier skill in the branch; synergies optional (could). A character cannot max everything, so it chooses a build.
    • B. As A but 2 points per level so a player can fill about half of the tree.
    • C. Flat list per class (no prerequisites), as in the earlier proxy answer QF-06-7.
  • Proposed default: A. Basic attack is free and not part of the 10.
  • Affects: R-202..R-208, R-014 (replaced), R-013 (points).

Q3 Respec rules

  • Why it matters: with three classes and 20-40 minute runs, players want to try builds; free respec also lowers the test cost for bots.
  • Options:
    • A. Skill points: unlimited and free in town; attributes are not respecced (LATER).
    • B. Skill points respec for gold, rising with level, in town.
    • C. One free respec at level 10, none after; or no respec at all (genre purist).
    • D. Delete and recreate the character is the only way (no feature).
  • Proposed default: A.
  • Affects: R-209, R-207, R-018.
